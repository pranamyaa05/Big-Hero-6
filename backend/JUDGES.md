# ReForm3D: notes for judges

ReForm3D turns "this everyday object is hard to use or broken" into **print-ready STL files**, for people with arthritis, tremor, low vision, one-handed use or limited mobility (UN SDG 3, 10 and 11).

An AI model writes the CAD code. A deterministic engine checks it, prints a picture of it for the model to look at, and builds the files. Thirty-six hand-tested accessibility templates are the building blocks the AI can reuse, and the offline fallback when no model answers.

## Two-minute demo script

1. **Start with a template request (about 3 seconds, always works).**
   "My mum can't turn the round door knob, arthritis, the knob is 5 cm across."
   Shows: request understood, a lever adapter sized to 5 cm, 3D preview, STL, print notes, print time and plastic from a real slicer run.
2. **Show a change without the AI.** Change a dimension (knob diameter, lever length) in the tuning fields. The part rebuilds in 1 to 3 seconds. Download the zip.
3. **Show the AI designing something that is not in the catalogue.** Use a request you pre-warmed (see below):
   "A hook to hang a guide dog leash by the front door at wheelchair height."
   Shows: the AI's stated understanding, its assumptions, the picture it looked at, the checks it passed or the warnings it could not clear.
4. **Show it saying no.**
   "A ramp that carries a 150 kg electric wheelchair up a 20 cm step."
   Load-bearing and safety parts are declined with a reason, rather than a weak plastic ramp being produced.
5. **Show the material switch.** Same request with the Material menu set to TPU: walls, overhang limits and print speed change.

## Before you go on stage

- **Pre-warm the demo requests with a strong model.** An identical request is served from the design cache in under a second. Use Gemini for this. The pool that passed the full test on 24 October: `gemini:2=gemini-3.5-flash, gemini:3=gemini-3.5-flash, gemini:2=gemini-3-flash-preview, gemini:3=gemini-3-flash-preview`, then free fallbacks (a new AI design took 39 s with it).
- **Order the pool best first.** Strong model first, free fallbacks after. `python -m app.cli models` shows what will be called and what was left out.
- **Keep Groq's free tier last or off.** Its 8,000 tokens per minute cannot fit a multi-step design.
- **Set `REFORM3D_PROMPT_LEAN=1`** for small-limit providers.
- Install **PrusaSlicer** on the demo machine for real print numbers (`REFORM3D_SLICER=auto`). Without it the numbers come from a calibrated estimate and the page says so.
- If the network or every model fails live, the offline planner still routes to a template. Say so; it is a designed behaviour, not a trick.

## What to expect from the AI part

Measured here on free models (details in the README, "Live results"): a design takes **3 to 7 minutes**, some requests fail because a free model times out or does not return code, and the shapes are plain. A strong model is faster and better. The honest framing: the *system* around the model (checks, failover, cache, fallback) is what we built and tested; the *model* is rented.

## Likely questions

**Is the CAD really generated by AI, or are these just templates?**
Both exist. An AI result says "Designed by <model>" and how many passes and fixes it took; a template result names the template. Anything the catalogue does not match goes to the AI, which writes manifold3d code. The templates are examples and fallbacks, not a menu.

**What stops the AI from producing junk or something unsafe?**
The code is parsed against an allow-list (no imports, no file or network access), run in a sandboxed subprocess with CPU, memory and time limits, then the mesh must be a closed solid with sensible volume, size, wall thickness and overhang. Failures go back to the model with the measurements. The model then looks at a render and may correct itself once. Load-bearing and medical-safety requests are declined.

**Will it hold?**
We do not do finite-element analysis. Strength comes from rules (load along layers, minimum walls, fillets, short cantilevers, material choice) given to the model and checked loosely. These are light-duty aids; do a test print first.

**How do you know it prints?**
Closed-solid, size and wall checks, an overhang survey, and a real PrusaSlicer 2.7.2 run on every template in the test suite (none above 25% support; parts labelled "no supports" under 3%). That audit found and fixed real overhangs in five templates. Nothing was physically printed in this project.

**Why PLA, PETG, TPU modes?**
Different materials have different minimum walls, bridging limits, speeds and densities. The request's material (or "flexible", "rubber") picks the profile, and it changes what is flagged and the estimate.

**What if the free AI is down?**
The pool tries the next model, waits out per-minute limits, and finally falls back to the template planner. A repeat of an earlier request is answered from cache.

**What does it cost to run?**
Free tiers only in this build. Free tiers change without notice and are rate limited; the README lists what was tested and what broke.

## Honest limits (short list)

- No strength simulation.
- Free models are slow and plain; failures happen.
- Overhang is estimated geometrically unless PrusaSlicer is installed.
- Not test-printed; dimensions are nominal.
- Docker image and Ollama were not built or run in the build environment (no daemon, no install).
- 36 templates; anything else depends on the AI.
- Single instance (SQLite, in-memory limits).
- Not a medical device.

Full detail: README.md, sections "What has and has not been tested" and "Honest limits".
